## Standard public-data request

Records Custodian:

We seek only information that may lawfully be released to the public in
compliance with all applicable laws. Please redact anything that cannot be
publicly released and provide the remaining data.

**We are requesting a response at no cost and do not authorize any charges.**
If producing a field, record, attachment, or dataset would result in a charge,
please redact or omit that portion and provide the remainder that can be supplied
for free. Document each cost-based omission using the availability and redaction
guidance below, identifying the affected data and explaining what would incur a
charge. If nothing can be supplied at no cost, please let us know without
incurring charges.

To avoid unnecessary delay, we expressly authorize you to redact or omit any
portions for which you would otherwise seek an Attorney General decision or
other legal ruling. Those portions are excluded from this request. Please
document the omissions using the redaction markers, reasons, and log described
below, and proceed with the remaining export without seeking a ruling on the
excluded portions, to the extent permitted by law.

**Our priority is prompt access to the data you can release. We would prefer
a redacted export now to a more complete export delayed by a ruling.** The
omitted details may not be needed for our work. If we later decide we need
any of that material, we will submit a separate request for it, and any
necessary ruling can be addressed in connection with that request.

Please provide publicly releasable data in your existing electronic export
formats. NIBRS exports and N-DEx-compatible data are also accepted. Please include
the exact format/profile, version, schema, and available documentation.
We use the [IACP-based checklist](#data-checklist) to identify relevant records,
not to require a particular export layout.

If a specification tailored to your system would make production easier or less
expensive, please respond with your full data model, including available tables,
fields, definitions, relationships, and export formats. We will submit a new
request that follows your native format as closely as possible.

Please provide an electronic copy of your agency's existing data catalog and
data model, including system and module inventories, table and field definitions,
schemas, entity-relationship diagrams, data dictionaries, code/lookup tables,
primary and foreign keys, cross-system identifier mappings, GIS layer definitions,
and existing export/API documentation.

For our initial request, please provide publicly releasable data and associated
records covering the preceding five years. For subsequent requests, please provide
any records added or changed since the date of our last request, including changes
to older records. Include current, historical, archived, closed, and legacy-system
records within the applicable period; available version/change history;
related documents and releasable attachments; and records maintained for the
agency by service providers. The data catalog and the underlying records are
separate requested deliverables. If you choose the tailored-request option
above, provide the full data model first so we can prepare the new data request.

This includes calls for service and dispatch; incidents, stops, searches,
citations, arrests and charges; investigations, prosecution referrals, and
available court/case outcomes and status history; booking and jail custody,
housing, movement, holds, bond, services, grievances, release and deaths;
use of force, injuries and pursuits; personnel identifiers and employment,
rank/title, salary/payroll, timesheets, overtime, education/training, FTO,
supervisory, complaint, commendation and discipline history; organizational
structure, beats, shifts and geographic shapes; task forces and agreements;
funding, grants, budgets, revenue, expenses, contracts, payments and assets;
policies, audits, and other maintained datasets. This list is illustrative,
not a limit on the requested releasable data.

Please provide existing native machine-readable exports with their documentation
and relationships. Existing standardized exchanges are welcome with their exact
profile, version and schema. There is no need to create a new database, compute
summaries, or rename native fields to match our model. For spatial data, please
include the shapes and coordinate reference system, not only centroids or maps
rendered as images. For records held by another custodian, please identify the
custodian and the relevant system or dataset if known.

If you do not have a requested dataset or field, please say so explicitly in an
availability file. A header-only CSV with an availability entry is welcome when
the columns are known; an entry without a file is enough otherwise. Distinguish
data you do not maintain, data with no records for the period, and data withheld
from release. Please do not silently omit a requested category.

Please include a file inventory identifying the source system/table, covered
dates, export time, record count, and available keys. Preserve relationships and
native identifiers to the extent releasable. Please provide releasable portions
of records that contain withheld information and identify which fields, records,
attachments, datasets, or periods were withheld, rather than representing them
as empty or nonexistent.

Where practical, mark a withheld field with `<redacted:reason-id>`, such as
`<redacted:R1>`, and define each reason ID separately in a redaction-reasons
file with your explanation and the authority relied upon. If your native export
uses a different redaction mechanism, please preserve that export and provide a
mapping or companion redaction log. The convention below can be used for fields,
whole records, files, attachments, or geometry. Please distinguish withholding
from data that was not collected, is unknown, is unavailable, or is held elsewhere.

We would also like quarterly updates containing records added or changed since
the date of our last request. Please recommend the best process for receiving these updates:
a new request each quarter, a recurring request, scheduled exports, or another
approach that works well for your agency.

The data is requested for analysis and publication on [PoliceConduct.org](https://PoliceConduct.org).
We are eager to partner with agencies in any way they would find useful. One option
is to help fulfill public data requests: by publishing data to our system periodically,
your agency could direct requesters to our site to obtain the published records
for free, reducing the work of responding to repeated requests for the same data.
